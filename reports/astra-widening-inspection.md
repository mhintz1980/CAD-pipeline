## 5. Engine ↔ bale-upright clearance (the user's reported concern)

Method, in order of strength:
1. **Triangle intersection (authoritative):** `BVHTree.overlap` of each upright's evaluated triangles against the whole engine triangle soup (2,276,902 tris) → **0 intersections on both uprights. There is no actual interference today.**
2. **X-slab lower bound (bbox window):** engine tri AABB *within the upright's YZ window* → minus 2.36 mm, plus 0.12 mm. This is a loose bound: it ignores the taper and the Y shape.
3. **X-slab lower bound (taper-aware, per 50 mm Z band using the upright's own Y extent):** **minus 9.46 mm**, **plus 9.62 mm**, both at Z 1167-1217 mm (upright Y ±205.5).
4. **Sampled point-to-triangle, both directions (upper bound):** **minus 13.559 mm**, **plus 22.959 mm**.
   * minus closest: upright (-665.43, -194.69, 1160.89) ↔ engine **COMPOUND.001** (-651.94, -194.62, 1162.20)
   * plus closest: upright (+795.80, -199.56, 1269.63) ↔ engine COMPOUND.001 (772.99, -199.42, 1272.30)
   * Limitation, stated in the JSON as `min_distance_method`: this is a **sampled upper bound** on the true separation, not an exact figure. The tight slab value (9.46 mm) is the stronger *lower* bound. No bbox overlap is being reported as a collision.

**True separation is therefore bracketed at roughly 9.5-13.6 mm (minus) and 9.6-23.0 mm (plus).** The engine is close but not touching.

Driving features / ranges (from `engine_X_envelope_by_Z_100mm`): the engine's widest band is **Z 1200-1400 mm, X -660.5 … +837.7** (it exceeds *both* uprights' inner faces there: -662.8 and +780.8). Both closest points sit at **Y ≈ -195 to -200 mm, Z ≈ 1161-1272 mm** — the exhaust/turbo-side protrusion of `COMPOUND.001`. The narrow bands are Z 1100-1600 mm on the minus side and Z 1000-1500 mm on the plus side; above Z ~1700 mm the engine retreats to X -413…-304 and could not clash.

**Expected benefit of the outward 76.2 mm translations:**

|---|---|---|---|
| minus upright | 13.559 / 9.46 mm | **89.759 mm** | +76.2 |

## 6. Hazards

**H1 (must fix) — `LBA-CB` is an X-spanning member, not a plus-side part.**
It is the bale top cross beam: X -941.26…+1040.19, Y ±9.53, Z +1608.9…+1913.7, 234 verts / 472 tris, closed manifold, and it mates **both** uprights at 0.000 mm. A naive "CAD_bale and centroid is +X → translate +76.2" rule (which is how the action table was auto-derived) would:
* break the `LBA-CB ↔ LBU-PP.001` joint by 76.2 mm,
* pull its minus end from -941.26 to -865.06, i.e. **26.36 mm short of the moved minus upright's outer face (-891.42)**, destroying the 126.04 mm end overhang that currently exists on the minus side,
* leave the plus-side overhang unchanged at 106.97 mm.
**Recommendation: extend both ends by 76.2 mm** (cut inside X[-842.19,-205.26] and X[303.74,+941.12], both 637 mm of feature-free straight section) — the same treatment as the other crossmembers. The tool's classifier has been corrected to test "spans both side-structure faces" **before** the CAD_bale membership test, and now emits `extend_ends_+76.2_each (X-spanning member)` with `action_note` "bridging member; do NOT rigid-translate" for `LBA-CB`.

**H2 (decision needed) — `DOUBLER` / `DOUBLER.001` touch a moving upright.**
Both are flat plates, X -711.2…+711.2, Z -483.39…-473.87 (sitting on the tray top plate `PP-FTT`), and `DOUBLER.001` shares a 0.000 mm corner interface with `LBU-PP.001` over X[-711.17,-662.82] × Y[245.78,292.08] × Z[-483.39,-473.87] (46.3 mm of Y engagement, 48.35 mm of X engagement). If the uprights translate ±76.2 and the doublers stay as classified, **that interface opens by 27.85 mm**. Options: (a) extend each doubler's outboard end by 76.2 mm — they are simple 9.5 mm plates with 151/406/151/406/151 mm feature-free bands, trivially extendable; (b) accept the split if the interface is incidental. **Astra's call**; evidence for either is in the JSON.

**H3 — the deck/end-rail plates are Family A (centre ≈ 0), not the datum.** `Part1^PP128-PP108-SKID(.001)`, `DOUBLER(.001)`, `PP-FBS..003` are centred on ~0 while the bale datum is +59.0. Widening "about the skid centre" by scaling or by moving the whole skid collection would shift them off the engine axis. Only per-part end-extension about the parts' own spans preserves both families.

**H4 — X-axis stretching would distort every hole, bore and fastener pattern.** A single uniform X scale to reach 2217.63 mm is 2217.63/2065.23 = **1.07378**:
* a 22 mm bolt hole → 23.62 mm (no longer M22; the bolt no longer fits),
* a 100 mm hole pitch → 107.38 mm (fastener pattern no longer matches the engine mounts or the vendor hardware),
* the 9.5 mm plate thicknesses and the 584 mm upright Y depths would also drift where the part is not aligned to X,
* and, because the fixed engine/pump must not move, any assembly-level scale would misalign them outright.
**Safer region-based alternative (recommended):** do not scale. For each part, cut at the verified feature-free X band and insert a straight extrusion of the local cross-section — 76.2 mm at each end for the X-spanning members, or a rigid translation for the outboard side structure and uprights. Local sections, holes, mating faces and plate thicknesses are bit-identical by construction, and the datum +59.0 mm is preserved because equal material is added on both sides.

**H5 — duplicate/near-coincident geometry.** `PP-FTS-1008.002` / `.004` and `.003` / `.005` are coincident duplicates (identical bbox to 0.01 mm), and `30938101_QUILT_2` / `_3` are duplicates in `CAD_pump_new` (fixed assembly, no action). Any per-part edit must hit **both** copies of each `PP-FTS-1008` pair or the result contains overlapping sheets. Checked: `PP-FBS/.001/.002/.003` are four distinct corner plates (different Y ends), not duplicates.

**H6 — the uprights are single closed manifolds** (0 boundary, 0 non-manifold, 0 wire edges, 1 component each), so there is no hidden loose geometry to leave behind when they translate. Same for `LBA-CB` (1 component, 234 v). The `PP-FTT` tray plates and the `PP-FTT`/`PP-FTS-1005` mating at 0.000 mm means the extension cut must stop at the plate's own boundary — do **not** cut into the rail overlap region.
